ROSMAP AREA PROJECT — STAGE 10A–D FINAL SIMULATION INTERPRETATION
Production multimodal ROSMAP-calibrated simulation results
Date: 2026-09-28

====================================================================================================
EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
====================================================================================================

Stage 10A–D used controlled simulations with known ground truth to ask when covariate-adjusted
Regular AREA detects disease-associated molecular signals differently from standard mean-based
methods.

Modalities:
    10A. Monocyte RNA: AREA vs DESeq2
    10B. DLPFC RNA: AREA vs DESeq2
    10C. DLPFC TMT proteomics: AREA vs limma
    10D. Cross-modality synthesis

Production design:
    25 replicates
    101 pre-specified signal scenarios
    4 signal features per scenario per replicate
    100 signal-feature instances per scenario per modality
    BH FDR threshold = 0.05
    frozen seed = 42
    no AREA block
    covariate-adjusted residual-df-corrected AREA inference

The central result is:

    AREA is NOT a generic detector of "heterogeneity," "outliers," or "extreme expression."

Instead, AREA is most sensitive when disease status produces a systematic directional change in
where cases fall in the ranked expression distribution.

The strongest AREA-specific signal in these simulations was an asymmetric, mean-preserving
multiplicative RNA heterogeneity regime. AREA was NOT sensitive to a purely symmetric increase in
dispersion.

Conventional mean-based methods were often more sensitive to sparse subset shifts, isolated
outliers, and one-tail amplification at low or moderate penetrance.

This refines, rather than simply rejects, the conceptual claims of the original AREA manuscript.

1. PRODUCTION CALIBRATION
====================================================================================================

Null p-value calibration was close to nominal for AREA in every modality:

Monocyte AREA:
    null p < 0.01 = 0.009682
    null p < 0.05 = 0.048958
    median null p = 0.500614

DLPFC AREA:
    null p < 0.01 = 0.010057
    null p < 0.05 = 0.050201
    median null p = 0.500076

TMT AREA:
    null p < 0.01 = 0.009902
    null p < 0.05 = 0.049869
    median null p = 0.498613

DLPFC DESeq2 and TMT limma were also close to nominal.

Monocyte DESeq2 showed reproducible mild anti-conservatism at the nominal-p level:
    null p < 0.01 = 0.012040
    null p < 0.05 = 0.055661
    median null p = 0.493011

This same pattern had already appeared in the three-replicate pilot.

    mean realized FDR = 0.0516
    median realized FDR = 0.0496
    maximum realized FDR = 0.0746

Monocyte AREA:
    mean discoveries/replicate = 86.56
    mean true discoveries = 82.96
    mean false discoveries = 3.60
    mean realized FDR = 0.0415
    median realized FDR = 0.0366
    maximum realized FDR = 0.0732

Monocyte DESeq2:
    mean discoveries/replicate = 122.16
    mean true discoveries = 112.28
    mean false discoveries = 9.88
    mean realized FDR = 0.0802
    median realized FDR = 0.0769
    maximum realized FDR = 0.1429

TMT AREA:
    mean discoveries/replicate = 96.88
    mean true discoveries = 92.68
    mean false discoveries = 4.20
    mean realized FDR = 0.0428
    median realized FDR = 0.0417
    maximum realized FDR = 0.0808

TMT limma:
    mean discoveries/replicate = 109.00
    mean true discoveries = 104.48
    mean false discoveries = 4.52
    mean realized FDR = 0.0409
    median realized FDR = 0.0392
    maximum realized FDR = 0.0654


Interpretation:

    AREA controlled mean realized FDR below 0.05 in all three modalities.

    DLPFC DESeq2 was approximately nominal overall.

    TMT limma was approximately nominal overall.

    Monocyte DESeq2 had materially elevated realized FDR (~0.08 rather than 0.05).

Important statistical note:

    The fraction of individual replicates with realized FDR > 0.05 is NOT itself a failure of FDR
    control. FDR is an expectation over repeated experiments, not a requirement that every replicate
    individually remain below 0.05.

    The meaningful concern is the mean monocyte DESeq2 realized FDR of ~0.08 across 25 replicates.

Consequence for interpretation:

    Any apparent monocyte DESeq2 power advantage must be interpreted with caution because DESeq2
    was operating at a more liberal realized error rate in this simulation.

    This does NOT invalidate the monocyte DESeq2 results, but it means raw FDR<0.05 power is not a
    perfectly equal-error-rate comparison in monocytes.

    AREA's strong monocyte advantage in the variance-only regime is especially notable because AREA
    achieved it while maintaining lower realized FDR than DESeq2.

====================================================================================================
3. BROAD MEAN SHIFTS
====================================================================================================

This is the standard differential-expression regime: most or all AD participants move in the same
direction.

DLPFC:
    0.5 SD:
        AREA power = 0.94
        DESeq2 power ≈ 0.96
    >= 1 SD:
        both methods essentially saturated at 1.00

Monocyte:
    0.5 SD:
        AREA = 0.43
        DESeq2 ≈ 0.57
    1 SD:
        AREA = 0.80
        DESeq2 ≈ 0.93
    2 SD:
        AREA = 0.95
        DESeq2 ≈ 0.99
    3 SD:
        both = 1.00

TMT:
    0.5 SD:
        AREA = 0.75
        limma ≈ 0.54
    >= 1 SD:
        both essentially saturated at 1.00

Interpretation:

    AREA remains fully capable of detecting ordinary directional mean shifts.

    It is not inherently inferior to mean-based methods under homogeneous effects.

    Weak-effect performance depends on modality.

====================================================================================================
4. RANDOM SUBSET SHIFTS
====================================================================================================

Only a fraction of AD participants receive the directional shift.

General pattern:

    At low penetrance (especially 5–20%), conventional mean-based methods often outperform AREA.

    At intermediate penetrance (around 30–50%), the gap narrows and becomes modality dependent.

    At high penetrance (75–100%), AREA usually catches up and both methods often approach saturation.

Examples:

20% penetrance, 2 SD:
    DLPFC:
        AREA = 0.16
        DESeq2 ≈ 0.42

    Monocyte:
        AREA = 0.05
        DESeq2 ≈ 0.35

    TMT:
        AREA = 0.10
        limma ≈ 0.17

30% penetrance, 2 SD:
    DLPFC:
        AREA = 0.81
        DESeq2 ≈ 0.89

    Monocyte:
        AREA = 0.25
        DESeq2 ≈ 0.47

    TMT:
        AREA = 0.46
        limma ≈ 0.62

50% penetrance, 2 SD:
    DLPFC:
        AREA = 0.98
        DESeq2 ≈ 0.96

    Monocyte:
        AREA = 0.69
        DESeq2 ≈ 0.71

    TMT:
        AREA = 0.99
        limma ≈ 1.00

Interpretation:

    AREA does not automatically gain power merely because a signal is incompletely penetrant.

    A relatively small shifted subgroup can be easier for a mean-based method to detect when the
    shift magnitude is large enough to influence the group mean.

====================================================================================================
5. ONE-TAIL AMPLIFICATION
====================================================================================================

This scenario deliberately takes AD participants already in the relevant expression tail and pushes
them farther outward.

General result:

    AREA was usually LESS sensitive than the conventional method at low or moderate penetrance.

Examples:

30% penetrance, 2 SD:
    DLPFC:
        AREA = 0.00
        DESeq2 ≈ 0.59

    Monocyte:
        AREA = 0.00
        DESeq2 ≈ 0.55

    TMT:
        AREA = 0.00
        limma ≈ 0.33

50% penetrance, 2 SD:
    DLPFC:
        AREA = 0.67
        DESeq2 ≈ 0.81

    Monocyte:
        AREA = 0.29
        DESeq2 ≈ 0.49

    TMT:
        AREA = 0.29
        limma = 1.00

At 75–100% penetrance, AREA generally catches up and can sometimes exceed the conventional method
at weak effect sizes.

Why?

    AREA operates on ranks.

    If someone is already close to the extreme end of the ranked list, making that person's raw
    expression much more extreme may change the actual expression value dramatically while changing
    the rank very little.

    Mean-based methods retain sensitivity to that magnitude change.

Therefore:

    "Extreme raw expression" is not synonymous with "strong AREA signal."

====================================================================================================
6. ISOLATED OUTLIERS
====================================================================================================

Only 1, 3, or 7 AD participants receive a large shift.

Result:

    AREA was essentially insensitive across these scenarios.

    Conventional methods occasionally detected these signals, especially monocyte DESeq2.

Examples:

Monocyte, 7 outliers:
    3 SD:
        AREA = 0.00
        DESeq2 ≈ 0.21

    4 SD:
        AREA = 0.00
        DESeq2 ≈ 0.21

Interpretation:

    Regular AREA is NOT an isolated-outlier detector.

    This is an important correction to an overly broad reading of the phrase "extreme expression."

====================================================================================================
7. SYMMETRIC TWO-TAIL SPREADING
====================================================================================================

This scenario makes the disease distribution more extreme at BOTH the low and high ends while
preserving the arithmetic center as closely as possible.

Result:

    Both AREA and the conventional methods were almost completely insensitive.

    AREA power was generally 0.00 and only occasionally reached ~0.01–0.03, even with very large
    effects and 100% penetrance.

Interpretation:

    Regular AREA is NOT a generic two-sample distributional test.

    A disease group can become much wider and have more extreme values in both directions without
    producing a directional concentration of disease labels in the rank ordering.

    In that situation, AREA has little signal to detect.

This is one of the cleanest mechanistic findings of Stage 10.

====================================================================================================
8. VARIANCE-ONLY / MEAN-PRESERVING HETEROGENEITY
====================================================================================================

The RNA and TMT results diverged sharply.

RNA simulation:
    multiplicative heterogeneity on the linear count scale
    finite-sample arithmetic mean multiplier normalized to exactly 1

DLPFC:
    0.5 SD:
        AREA = 0.00
        DESeq2 = 0.00

    1 SD:
        AREA = 0.02
        DESeq2 = 0.00

    2 SD:
        AREA = 0.45
        DESeq2 = 0.00

    3 SD:
        AREA = 0.82
        DESeq2 ≈ 0.03

Monocyte:
    0.5 SD:
        AREA = 0.01
        DESeq2 ≈ 0.01

    1 SD:
        AREA = 0.19
        DESeq2 = 0.00

    2 SD:
        AREA = 0.96
        DESeq2 ≈ 0.22

    3 SD:
        AREA = 0.99
        DESeq2 ≈ 0.50

TMT:
    0.5 SD:
        AREA = 0.00
        limma = 0.00

    1 SD:
        AREA = 0.01
        limma = 0.00

    2 SD:
        AREA = 0.00
        limma = 0.00

    3 SD:
        AREA = 0.00
        limma = 0.00


Interpretation:

    AREA does NOT simply detect variance changes.

    The RNA generator creates mean-preserving multiplicative heterogeneity. Although the arithmetic
    mean multiplier is exactly 1, the induced distribution on the expression scale is asymmetric:
    many observations can move modestly in one direction while fewer observations move strongly in
    the opposite direction.

    That can create a directional rearrangement of rank positions even though the arithmetic mean is
    preserved.

    AREA detects that rank redistribution.

    In contrast, the TMT variance-only simulation uses a symmetric additive zero-mean perturbation on
    the log2 scale. That increases spread without creating the same directional rank footprint.

    AREA has essentially no power there.

Therefore, the correct statement is NOT:

    "AREA detects variance better."

A better statement is:

    "AREA can be sensitive to mean-preserving heterogeneity when that heterogeneity induces an
    asymmetric directional redistribution of ranks, but it is not sensitive to purely symmetric
    increases in dispersion."

====================================================================================================
9. WHAT THIS MEANS FOR STAGE 9
====================================================================================================

Stage 9 showed that AREA-predominant ROSMAP features generally had lower NCI/AD distributional
overlap than conventional-method-predominant features.

Stage 10 shows that lower overlap alone is NOT sufficient to explain AREA selection.

Why?

    A symmetric increase in variance or symmetric two-tail spreading can reduce distributional
    overlap substantially.

    Yet AREA showed essentially no power for those symmetric geometries.

Therefore, the Stage 9 AREA-predominant features are more plausibly characterized by:

    directional or asymmetric rank redistribution

rather than simply:

    broader variance
    heavier tails
    isolated outliers
    or lower overlap in a generic sense

This is a substantially sharper mechanistic interpretation of the Stage 9 geometry result.

====================================================================================================
10. COMPARISON WITH THE ORIGINAL AREA MANUSCRIPT
====================================================================================================

The original AREA manuscript describes AREA as a rank-based method for detecting disease
associations with high or low expression.

That core concept is supported.

However, Stage 10 places important limits on a broad interpretation of "extreme expression."

Original manuscript simulation structure:
    - approximately 400 pseudo-individuals
    - normally distributed expression values calibrated from HTP genes
    - affected distributions shifted by 1, 2, or 3 SD
    - variable penetrance produced by mixing disease-labeled participants from a shifted affected
      distribution with disease-labeled participants from the unaffected distribution
    - AREA evaluated for recovery of simulated gene-label associations

This design is conceptually closest to the Stage 10 RANDOM_SUBSET_SHIFT scenario.

The original manuscript reported that AREA could recover associations with relatively few affected
individuals and across a broad range of affected proportions.

Stage 10 partly corroborates that conclusion:
    - AREA does recover incomplete-penetrance directional signals
    - performance improves strongly as penetrance and/or effect size increase
    - at high penetrance, AREA often approaches or matches conventional methods

But Stage 10 also qualifies it:
    - under ROSMAP-calibrated RNA/TMT distributions, low-penetrance directional signals can be hard
      for AREA
    - DESeq2/limma often outperform AREA at 5–30% penetrance
    - isolated outliers are not an AREA strength
    - making already-extreme individuals more extreme is not necessarily an AREA strength
    - symmetric two-tail dispersion is not an AREA strength

These results do NOT directly invalidate the original simulations because the designs differ
substantially.

Important design differences:

Original AREA simulations:
    Normal-expression pseudo-individuals
    simplified feature sets
    original unadjusted AREA enrichment/permutation framework
    primary question: can AREA recover the known association?

Current Stage 10:
    ROSMAP-calibrated negative-binomial RNA counts
    empirical-bootstrap TMT distributions
    full modality-wide feature universes
    realistic sample sizes and covariates
    covariate-adjusted FWL partial-rank AREA
    residual-df-corrected inference
    direct DESeq2 / limma comparators
    primary question: where does AREA outperform, match, or underperform conventional methods?

Therefore, direct numerical power comparisons between the original manuscript and Stage 10 should
not be treated as apples-to-apples.

The main conceptual refinement is:

    AREA is best understood as a detector of directional enrichment in expression ranks.

It should NOT be described broadly as a detector of any kind of extreme-expression heterogeneity.

====================================================================================================
11. MONOCYTE DESEQ2 CALIBRATION CAVEAT
====================================================================================================

Monocyte DESeq2 deserves explicit caution.

Production:
    null p < 0.05 = 0.055661
    mean realized FDR = 0.080174
    median realized FDR = 0.076923
    23/25 replicates had realized FDR > 0.05

This means the nominal FDR<0.05 DESeq2 threshold was relatively liberal under this particular
monocyte simulation generator.

Consequences:

    1. Monocyte DESeq2 power advantages should not automatically be interpreted as pure sensitivity
       advantages.

    2. Some fraction of the higher discovery rate reflects weaker empirical error control.

    3. This caveat is much less important for DLPFC DESeq2 and TMT limma, which were near nominal.

    4. AREA's monocyte variance-only advantage remains notable because AREA had BOTH higher power in
       that regime and lower realized FDR overall.

This issue should be carried forward in every Stage 10 figure or manuscript-level comparison.

It should NOT be "fixed" by altering the already-frozen primary simulation after seeing results.

Any equal-error-rate or recalibrated comparison should be explicitly labeled as a secondary
sensitivity analysis.

====================================================================================================
12. WHAT STAGE 10 DOES AND DOES NOT ESTABLISH
====================================================================================================

Supported:

    - AREA inference is well calibrated under the frozen production simulation.
    - AREA can robustly detect standard broad directional effects.
    - sparse/subset effects do not inherently favor AREA.
    - isolated outliers do not favor AREA.
    - symmetric dispersion does not favor AREA.
    - asymmetric mean-preserving multiplicative RNA heterogeneity can strongly favor AREA.
    - modality matters.
    - Stage 9's lower-overlap AREA signature cannot be explained by generic variance alone.

Not supported:

    - AREA is universally more powerful than DESeq2 or limma.
    - AREA is a generic heterogeneity detector.
    - AREA is a generic outlier detector.
    - AREA detects any reduction in case/control distribution overlap.
    - the current covariate-adjusted AREA behaves identically to the original unadjusted AREA.
    - one simulation geometry proves that Stage 9 empirical genes follow that exact biological
      mechanism.

====================================================================================================
FINAL INTERPRETIVE STATEMENT
====================================================================================================

A concise defensible summary is:

    "ROSMAP-calibrated simulations show that covariate-adjusted AREA is not a generic detector of
    extreme values or distributional heterogeneity. It is most sensitive to disease-associated
    changes that produce directional or asymmetric redistribution in expression ranks. Conventional
    mean-based methods generally retain an advantage for sparse subset shifts, isolated outliers,
    and one-tail amplification at low to moderate penetrance, whereas AREA shows a strong advantage
    for asymmetric, mean-preserving multiplicative heterogeneity in RNA. Purely symmetric increases
    in dispersion are essentially undetected by AREA. These results refine the original AREA
    manuscript's extreme-expression framework and indicate that the lower NCI/AD distributional
    overlap observed among AREA-predominant ROSMAP features in Stage 9 likely reflects directional
    rank redistribution rather than generic variance or outlier structure. AREA maintained mean
    realized FDR near 0.04 across all three modalities; monocyte DESeq2 showed elevated realized FDR
    (~0.08), so monocyte power comparisons require additional caution."
